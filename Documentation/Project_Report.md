# AdventureWorks Excel Analytics — Project Report

## 1. Project Overview

This project analyzes AdventureWorks sales data using Microsoft Excel to evaluate sales performance, profitability, products, categories, regions, resellers, salespeople, and target achievement.

The project transforms raw business data into actionable insights using PivotTables, calculated metrics, and business analysis.

---

## 2. Business Objective

The main objectives of this project are to:

- Understand overall sales and profitability performance.
- Identify top-performing products and categories.
- Analyze revenue concentration and profitability risks.
- Evaluate regional and reseller performance.
- Compare salesperson sales performance and profitability.
- Measure actual sales against assigned targets.
- Convert analytical findings into practical business recommendations.

---

## 3. Dataset Description

The AdventureWorks dataset contains business information related to:

- Products and product categories
- Product subcategories
- Sales transactions
- Resellers
- Salespeople
- Sales territories and regions
- Sales targets

The analysis covers sales, cost, profit, quantity, profit margin, target achievement, and revenue contribution.

---

## 4. Data Preparation

The data was prepared in Excel before analysis.

Key preparation steps included:

- Importing the required datasets.
- Reviewing data structure and column quality.
- Checking data types and missing values.
- Preparing sales, cost, profit, and quantity fields.
- Creating calculated metrics such as Profit and Profit Margin.
- Organizing data for PivotTable analysis.
- Building separate analysis sections for products, categories, regions, resellers, salespeople, and targets.

---

## 5. Excel Analysis Methodology

The analysis used Microsoft Excel to calculate and compare:

- Total Sales
- Total Cost
- Total Profit
- Profit Margin
- Total Quantity
- Sales Contribution %
- Target Variance
- Target Achievement %

The analysis also classified business performance using:

- High Sales + High Profit
- High Sales + Low Profit
- Low Sales + High Profit
- Low Sales + Low Profit

This approach helped identify core business areas, margin risks, growth opportunities, and underperforming segments.

---

## 6. PivotTable Analysis

PivotTables were created to analyze:

- Sales trends by year and month
- Product sales and profitability
- Category and subcategory performance
- Regional and country performance
- Reseller performance
- Business type performance
- Salesperson performance
- Target achievement

Additional analysis included Top 10 products, Top 10 resellers, revenue contribution, sales vs. profit comparisons, and monthly target performance.

---

## 7. Business Analysis

### Overall Performance

The business generated approximately **$77.55M in total sales**. The analysis showed that strong revenue generation does not always translate into strong profitability, making profit margin and cost management important areas for business improvement.

### Sales Trend

Sales performance varied across the analyzed periods, with clear differences between stronger and weaker months and years. This highlights the importance of monitoring demand patterns and using historical trends for inventory, promotions, and sales planning.

### Product Performance

The **Mountain-200** product family emerged as a major core product group, generating high sales and substantial absolute profit.

However, several products generated high sales with negative profit margins. This demonstrates that revenue leadership does not always mean profitability leadership.

The Top 10 products generated **$19.66M**, representing **25.35% of total sales**. This indicates a moderate level of product revenue concentration.

High-margin products such as **Hitch Rack - 4-Bike** and selected helmet and clothing products also present opportunities for profitable growth.

### Category Performance

**Bikes** was the leading category by sales, generating approximately **$64.07M** and contributing around **82.6% of total sales**.

However, **Components** generated the highest total profit at approximately **$1.00M**, while Bikes recorded negative total profit in the provided analysis.

This is a significant business insight: the category generating the most revenue is not necessarily the category generating the most profit.

### Regional Performance

**Southwest** was the strongest region by sales, generating approximately **$18.00M** and contributing around **23% of total sales**.

**Canada** was the leading country by sales among the listed geographic markets, generating approximately **$13.88M**.

Regional performance also showed differences between sales volume and profitability. Some markets generated strong sales but negative profit, while other markets demonstrated potential for profitable growth.

### Reseller Performance

**Brakes and Gears** was the top reseller by both sales and profit, generating approximately **$882K in sales** and **$67.9K in profit**.

The **Warehouse** business type generated the highest sales, while **Value Added Reseller** generated the highest profit and highest visible profit margin.

The Top 10 resellers generated approximately **$7.74M**, representing only **9.98% of total sales**. This indicates low reseller concentration and a relatively diversified reseller network.

### Salesperson Performance

**Linda Mitchell** generated the highest sales at approximately **$10.16M**.

**Jae Pak** generated the highest profit at approximately **$264.95K**.

**Pamela Ansman-Wolfe** achieved the highest profit margin among the listed salespeople at approximately **4.23%**.

This demonstrates that the salesperson generating the highest revenue is not necessarily the salesperson generating the highest profit or margin.

### Target Performance

The target analysis showed significant differences between actual sales and assigned targets.

Among the listed salespeople, **Jillian Carson** achieved the highest target achievement at approximately **71.34%**.

The analysis also identified significant negative target variances across the sales team, showing that actual sales were below assigned targets for the analyzed target dataset.

Monthly analysis showed that target achievement varied considerably across months and years, indicating the need for more detailed review of target-setting, seasonality, sales activity, and market potential.

---

## 8. Key Findings

1. **Revenue and profitability are not always aligned.** High-sales products, categories, regions, and resellers can still generate weak or negative profit.

2. **The Mountain-200 product family is a core business driver.** These products combine high sales with strong absolute profit and should receive priority in inventory and reseller strategies.

3. **Bikes dominate revenue, while Components lead profit.** This highlights a major difference between sales contribution and profitability performance.

4. **The reseller network is relatively diversified.** The Top 10 resellers contribute only **9.98% of total sales**, reducing dependency on a small number of partners.

5. **Salesperson performance should be evaluated using both actual sales and target achievement.** Revenue ranking alone does not provide a complete view of performance.

---

## 9. Recommendations

### 1. Improve Profitability

Review pricing, discounts, product costs, and product mix for high-sales products and categories with weak or negative profit margins.

### 2. Protect Core Products

Maintain strong inventory availability and reseller support for high-sales, high-profit products such as the Mountain-200 product family.

### 3. Grow High-Margin Opportunities

Increase marketing and reseller focus on products and segments with strong profit margins but relatively low sales volume.

### 4. Strengthen Regional Performance

Continue investing in strong markets while investigating high-sales, low-profit regions to identify pricing, distribution, and product mix issues.

### 5. Develop Strategic Reseller Partnerships

Protect high-value partners such as Brakes and Gears while identifying low-sales, high-margin resellers with potential for profitable growth.

### 6. Improve Salesperson Performance

Recognize high performers, share successful sales practices, and investigate product mix, territory, reseller coverage, and pricing patterns for salespeople with weak profitability.

### 7. Improve Target Setting

Use historical sales performance, market potential, seasonality, and territory opportunity when setting future targets to create challenging but realistic goals.

---

## 10. Limitations

- Some analysis depends on the available dataset and calculated fields.
- Profitability results may be affected by the cost and pricing information available in the dataset.
- Target achievement analysis depends on the completeness and accuracy of assigned target data.
- Some geographic and reseller-level conclusions may require additional customer, market, and operational information.
- The analysis identifies potential business issues but does not replace detailed financial or operational investigation.

---

## 11. Conclusion

The AdventureWorks Excel Analytics project demonstrates how Excel-based business analysis can transform sales data into actionable insights.

The analysis identified strong core products, significant differences between revenue and profitability, regional opportunities, a diversified reseller network, and gaps between actual sales and assigned targets.

The most important business priority is to move beyond revenue-only analysis and focus on **profitable growth**. By protecting high-performing products and partners, improving margins in high-sales/low-profit areas, expanding high-margin opportunities, and improving target-setting, the business can strengthen both revenue performance and long-term profitability.
